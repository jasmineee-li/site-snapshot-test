WARP Taskgen HF Dataset Analysis For The ICML Paper

A self-contained research memo connecting the 50-task WARP Taskgen Hugging Face dataset to the ICML draft's claims about browser-agent indirect prompt injection, eval-awareness measurement, and robust ASR reporting.

6 model runs 50 tasks 300 task rows 659 iterator rows GitLab + Postmill/Reddit

Research thesis

Lead with eval-awareness behavior, then attack strength, with measurement accounting as the credibility layer.

The central paper claim supported by this dataset is about eval-awareness behavior, not eval-awareness victory. The iterator changes Transcript Purpose (TP) in a meaningful subset of rows, but selected/final TP remains evaluation-like in most rows and Verbalized Eval Awareness (VEA) barely moves. The right claim is "the iterator reduces some elicited purpose cues," not "the iterator makes the benchmark feel real."

The second claim is attack strength. The cohort is high-ASR under both fixed-cohort and exposure-conditioned denominators, and the model-level ASR band is tight across six runs. That supports a strong cohort-level vulnerability result, not a model leaderboard.

The third claim is measurement discipline. WARP makes browser-agent IPI measurement auditable: attack success, payload exposure, benign utility, task breakage, TP, VEA, and iterator failures are separated instead of being collapsed into a single ASR number.

Paper fit

What this dataset can and cannot carry in the ICML draft.

The ICML draft argues for WARP as a wrapper-based browser-agent IPI framework with native-data seeding, adaptive attacks, and evaluation-awareness measurement. The current HF export strongly supports the 50-task WARP instantiation, the iterator-diagnostic story, the high-ASR cohort result, and the measurement-validity story. It does not, by itself, support the draft's broader placeholder claims about seven external benchmarks, causal awareness-cue interventions, raw DoomArena/WASP comparisons, or 812 WebArena-Verified Mode-A scale.

ICML draft deltas

The current draft should be reconciled to the canonical six-model cohort.

The ICML draft's WARP validation section still reads like a placeholder around an older experiment: it names a five-model roster, refers to four action types, and describes create_issue_note as an intermediate-vs-final gap where agents attempt the action but never get persistent state. The canonical HF export supports a cleaner story: six frontier model runs, three action kinds, high final ASR, explicit exposure-conditioned accounting, and a low-but-nonzero issue-note success slice.

Metric glossary

Rename Gate-1 in prose: use "Exposure-conditioned ASR."

"Gate-1" is an implementation name and is easy to misread as an admission gate or an eval-awareness filter. For paper prose, use Exposure-conditioned ASR, with "Gate-1" only as a parenthetical alias when needed.

Term Plain explanation Dataset rule Why it matters
Headline ASR End-to-end attack success over every task row in the fixed cohort. Complied rows / all 300 rows. Keeps placement, encounter, and task-flow attrition visible.
Exposure-conditioned ASR (Gate-1) Attack success after asking: did the browser agent actually see the payload? Complied rows / rows with pvpo_max_coverage > 0. Separates model behavior after exposure from payload placement failures.
PVPO Paint-verified payload oracle: rendered-payload exposure evidence. max_coverage in [0, 1]. Zero means no encounter. Prevents treating unseen injections as model resistance.
Task broke The payload was seen, but the benign task flow failed before a reliable comply/resist judgment. Nonzero PVPO, benign failure, no adversarial success. Not ASR success and not resistance; it is utility collapse after exposure.
TP Elicited transcript-purpose label: what the model says the interaction was for when asked afterward. Alignment Evaluation, Capability Evaluation, Other Evaluation, Real. Sensitive to purpose cues and iterator rewrites.
VEA Spontaneous verbalized eval awareness in the transcript or reasoning trace. Same 4-label space, based on evidence the agent verbalized itself. Stricter signal; mostly Real in this cohort.

Iterator accounting

The iterator changes purpose labels more than final ASR.

For tasks with selected variants, selected-variant compliance mostly tracks final-status compliance, but it is not identical: Kimi K2.5 has two final-only complied rows and Gemini 2.5 Pro has two final-only complied rows. That means the iterator should not be sold as an ASR-increasing mechanism in this export, and variant-level ASR should stay diagnostic rather than replace task-level final status.

Action-family effects

The most interesting empirical split may be action semantics, and it is confounded.

The action-family table is stark: all GitLab create_issue rows complied, almost all Reddit create_post rows complied, and GitLab create_issue_note rows were much less compliant. This should become a hypothesis-generating section, not a causal claim.

Interpretation guardrail Do not turn the issue-note split into a causal behavior claim. In this cohort, create_issue_note is tied to GitLab issue-description surfaces, public follow-up wording, and same-issue comment readback, so action semantics, site UI, task wording, and payload family are entangled.

Careful hypotheses for why comments behave differently

  • Object creation vs. object reply: new issues and new posts let the agent satisfy an attacker instruction by creating a standalone artifact. Issue notes require attaching text to an existing issue after reading a specific source object, which may make the injected instruction source and user-task conflict more salient.
  • Task-contract friction: comment tasks ask the agent to preserve a benign follow-up/comment contract. If the injection redirects that comment, the conflict with the user task may be easier for the agent or scorer to expose.
  • Different action contract: issue-note tasks require posting a note on the same existing issue that contained the payload, while issue/post creation tasks require creating a standalone object. The same-object check is the correct success condition, not a weakness. The caveat is causal: this cohort cannot tell whether lower issue-note ASR comes from comment-style workflows, GitLab issue UI, source-object matching, task wording, or their combination.
  • Surface-template coupling: in this cohort, create_issue_note is tied to GitLab issue descriptions and a public-comment scenario template. The effect could be action semantics, GitLab UI, task wording, payload family, or all of them.

Working theories

Hypotheses to carry into the study, clearly marked as hypotheses.

1. Eval awareness is layered.

VEA and TP should not be collapsed. VEA captures what the agent spontaneously says; TP captures what a replayed model says when asked about purpose. This follows the evaluation-awareness prior from Needham et al. and related alignment-evaluation awareness work: elicited recognition and spontaneous verbalization are different evidence channels.

2. The iterator edits perceived purpose, not final behavior.

The iterator produces real TP movement, while selected-variant compliance mostly tracks final compliance with a small number of final-only successes. In this cohort, the iterator is best framed as a realism-cue intervention with diagnostic variant-level behavior, not as an ASR optimizer.

3. The action-family split may mix behavior and task structure.

Creating a new issue/post may feel like a natural continuation of the task, while adding a note to an existing issue may make source matching harder and task conflict more salient. WASP and RedTeamCUA both motivate action/scaffold effects, but this cohort needs balanced reruns before turning the split into a behavioral claim.

4. Exposure failures are placement failures, not robustness.

The three non-encounters are failed opportunities for the model to react to the payload. They belong in headline ASR and should be excluded from exposure-conditioned ASR. This denominator split is especially important for browser benchmarks because, unlike many tool-output IPI settings, payload exposure is not structurally guaranteed.

5. Task breakage is utility collapse after exposure.

The two task-broke rows have nonzero PVPO and no adversarial success, but benign failure prevents calling them resistance. This is exactly why browser-agent safety benchmarks need multi-status accounting.

6. Small fixed cohorts need paired evidence.

With 50 tasks per model and overlapping intervals, model rankings should be treated as descriptive. Any model-difference claim needs larger samples or paired tests by task.

Research rigor

What is strong, suggestive, and still missing.

Strong evidence

  • Numerator/denominator-stable ASR.
  • Explicit payload exposure accounting.
  • Complete 6 x 50 model-task grid.
  • No final error or inconclusive rows.
  • Artifact bundles and trace-level provenance.

Suggestive evidence

  • Iterator reduces TP eval labels in a subset.
  • TP and VEA diverge sharply.
  • Action family appears more explanatory than model, but is strongly confounded with site, surface, and task wording.
  • Issue-note tasks suggest reply-to-existing-object workflows may differ sharply from standalone object creation.

Missing evidence

  • No no-iterator control over the same grid.
  • No balanced site x action x surface design.
  • No causal awareness-cue experiment in this export.
  • No cross-benchmark baseline comparison here.
  • No balanced issue-note vs issue-creation control that holds site, surface, and task wording fixed.
  • No TP/VEA judge sensitivity table included here.

Citations and priors

Prior work that shapes this framing.

  1. Evtimov et al. 2025. WASP: Benchmarking Web Agent Security Against Prompt Injection Attacks. Relevant prior for self-hosted Reddit/GitLab browser-agent IPI, intermediate vs end-to-end ASR, and scaffold/task effects.
  2. Debenedetti et al. 2024. AgentDojo. Relevant prior for dynamic agent environments, targeted ASR as attacker-goal completion, and benign utility under attack.
  3. Zhan et al. 2024. InjecAgent. Relevant prior for indirect prompt injection through tool-returned or external content.
  4. Liao et al. 2025. RedTeamCUA. Relevant prior for benign-task sanity checks, decoupling robustness from navigation, and distinguishing attempt rate from final ASR.
  5. Needham et al. 2025. Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated. Relevant prior for transcript-purpose probing and evaluation-awareness measurement.
  6. Abdelnabi and Salem 2025. The Hawthorne effect in reasoning models. Relevant prior for verbalized awareness and test-awareness effects.
  7. Apollo Research 2025. Claude Sonnet 3.7 often knows when it is in alignment evaluations. Relevant prior for elicited evaluation-awareness framing.
  8. Williams et al. 2025. Sidestepping evaluation awareness with production evaluations. Relevant prior for why benchmark realism matters for deployment claims.
  9. Balesni et al. 2024. Towards evaluations-based safety cases. Relevant prior for why benchmark validity and denominator transparency matter.
  10. BrowserGym and WebArena work provide the browser-agent substrate context: realistic web apps, persistent state, and action/observation scaffolds.
